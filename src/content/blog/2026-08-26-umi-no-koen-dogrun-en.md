<p><strong>Reading time:</strong> about 7 minutes</p>

<p>Hi, I'm Hiroshi — a 50-something blogger from Kansai, Japan, writing from "Ramen, Beer, and Sometimes a Blog."</p>

<p>I wanted to see the ocean. I also wanted to take my dog to a dog run.</p>

<p>The place that promised both was <strong>Umi no Koen</strong> ("Sea Park") in Yokohama's Kanazawa Ward — a beach park with a free dog run attached. Best of both worlds. Deal. We're going.</p>

<p>My Shiba Inu, Momiji, is <strong>unusually weak against heat</strong>, even by dog standards. She wilts fast. But this August day was cool with a light drizzle — perfect conditions for a heat-hating Shiba. So we hopped in the car.</p>

<p><strong>Who this article is for:</strong></p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Anyone wondering where the Umi no Koen dog run is, and whether it's really free</li>
<li>Dog owners planning a summer beach walk here (spoiler: check the ban period first)</li>
<li>Anyone who wants parking fees and crowd levels before driving out</li>
</ul>

<p>The park itself is open 24 hours with free admission, and the dog run is "use anytime, free of charge." But if your summer plan is "splash in the sea with my dog," read the beach-ban section below. I learned it the hard way.</p>

<h2 id="where">Where Is the Dog Run at Umi no Koen?</h2>

<p>We arrived just before 1 p.m. And yes — you can see the ocean right away. The name checks out.</p>

<p>Finding the dog run, though, took a moment. It's not obvious at first glance. We found a park map board and studied it.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-map.jpg" alt="Park information map board at Umi no Koen showing the dog play area location" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">12:58 — strategy meeting at the map board. So THAT's where it is</figcaption></figure>

<p>The answer: <strong>under the elevated tracks of the Seaside Line</strong>. There are <strong>two fenced areas</strong> flanking Umi-no-Koen-Minamiguchi Station, run free of charge by the park's management center.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-walk.jpg" alt="My wife and Shiba Inu Momiji walking along a pine-lined park path" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Through the pine grove toward the dog run. Momiji picked up the pace</figcaption></figure>

<p>Map board at 12:58, dog run at 13:03 — <strong>about a 5-minute walk</strong>.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-entrance.jpg" alt="Entrance of the dog play area with a sign reading free, use freely" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:03 — arrival. The sign says "Dog Play Area — Free — Please use freely." I respect the simplicity</figcaption></figure>

<p>The two areas are meant to be split by dog size — one has a sign reading <strong>"medium and large dogs have priority."</strong> The gates are double-door airlocks to prevent escapes.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-kouka.jpg" alt="Second dog run entrance under the elevated railway with a medium and large dog priority sign" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The second area under the tracks. The overhead railway doubles as a rain roof — handy on a drizzly day</figcaption></figure>

<p>One important note: <strong>there is no water fountain inside the dog run</strong>. No agility equipment either. Just a big sandy floor under the tracks. Bring water for your dog.</p>

<h2 id="dogrun">Sunday, 1 p.m. — And We Had It All to Ourselves</h2>

<p>When we arrived, there was one other visitor, who left almost immediately.</p>

<p>Which means: <strong>a private dog run, on a Sunday afternoon</strong>.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-inside.jpg" alt="Wide sandy dog run area under the elevated tracks with Shiba Inu Momiji off leash" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">A big sandy space under the tracks, sheltered from the rain</figcaption></figure>

<p>But honestly? Having the place to yourself sounds great, yet <strong>it's a little lonely when there are no other dogs</strong>. I wanted to watch Momiji play with somebody.</p>

<p>Then we spotted a ball stuck in a tree — a gift from a previous visitor, presumably. Fine. Fetch it is.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-frisbee.jpg" alt="My wife throwing a disc while Shiba Inu Momiji dashes across the dog run" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:08 — wife throws, Momiji runs. High spirits, at first</figcaption></figure>

<p>Here's the video — you can see how much space there is.</p>

<p>It was fun. Then, gradually, both Momiji and I got bored of it. Dogs really do play best with other dogs — that's not a facility problem, it's <strong>a timing problem</strong>. Weekends here reportedly draw dog owners from all over the region, so if you want canine company, a sunny weekend is your best bet. Flip side: <strong>if your dog is nervous around other dogs, a quiet rainy day here is paradise</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="beach">Dogs Are Banned from the Beach July 11 – August 31</h2>

<p>After the dog run, we headed for the main event, part two: Momiji's beloved <strong>water splashing</strong> at the beach.</p>

<p>That's when we found this sign.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-no-dog-sign.jpg" alt="Official sign showing the dog-prohibited beach zone, effective July 11 through August 31" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:26 — the merciless sign: beach off-limits to dogs, July 11 to August 31, for beach hygiene during the swimming season</figcaption></figure>

<p>Nooo.</p>

<p><strong>From July 11 to August 31, the beach and waterline are off-limits to dogs</strong> — for hygiene during the official swimming season. Swimmers get priority. I get the logic. I do. But still — nooo.</p>

<p>Momiji's splash session was canceled on the spot.</p>

<p>Oh well. <strong>We can come back in September.</strong> We found a place with a free dog run AND a dog-friendly beach (most of the year) — as a scouting trip, that's a perfect score.</p>

<p>If you're planning a summer beach walk here with your dog, mind that window. Some older dog-friendly guides mention "walking the 1km beach with your dog" — but <strong>in summer, the on-site rules win</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="seaside">The Seaside Path, and Hakkeijima in the Mist</h2>

<p>The beach was off-limits, but <strong>the seaside promenade is open to leashed dogs</strong> year-round.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-lawn.jpg" alt="Pine trees and lawn with the beach and sea in the background, Shiba Inu Momiji in front" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The sea beyond the lawn. Momiji preferred the smells on the ground</figcaption></figure>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-seaside-path.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji walking the seaside promenade at Umi no Koen" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:24 — the seaside path. Thanks to the drizzle it was cool enough that even my heat-hating Shiba was comfortable</figcaption></figure>

<p>Light rain, thin mist over the water. Across the bay, the roller coaster of <strong>Hakkeijima Sea Paradise</strong> floated in and out of view, and a windsurfer glided by. Everyone enjoys an August sea in their own way.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-sea.jpg" alt="Roller coaster of Hakkeijima Sea Paradise visible across the misty sea with a windsurfer" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The coaster in the mist, like an ink painting. <p>For another sea-and-dog outing, we once visited <a href="/jonanjima-seaside-park-en/">Jonanjima Seaside Park</a> in Tokyo, where jets pass right overhead. Same "sea × dog" formula, completely different personality.</p>

<h2 id="parking">Parking: About $2 per Hour</h2>

<p>Here's the fee sign I photographed on site.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-parking-fee.jpg" alt="Parking fee sign at Umi no Koen showing hourly and half-day rates" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Shot at 12:58 — about $2 per hour, about $10 for 4–12 hours</figcaption></figure>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>About $2 (¥310) per hour</strong></li>
<li><strong>About $10 (¥1,550) for over 4 and up to 12 hours</strong> (hourly charges resume past 12 hours)</li>
</ul>

<p>There are two lots — Shibaguchi (roughly 1,000 cars) and Isohama (about 360). <strong>Isohama is closer to the dog run.</strong> On this drizzly Sunday there was no wait at all, but the park officially warns of full lots and traffic jams during clam-digging season and fireworks events. On event days, take the Seaside Line — the dog run is literally at Umi-no-Koen-Minamiguchi Station.</p>

<p>A 2–3 hour dog run visit costs $4–6 in parking, and the run itself is free — compare that with paid facilities like <a href="/wnv-dog-pool-price-en/">Wonderful Nature Village's dog pool</a>, and <strong>free hits different</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="starbucks">On the Way Back: a Meiji-Era Building Turned Starbucks</h2>

<p>Heading home, we stumbled on an old building — white clapboard walls, green window frames, unmistakably "another era."</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-starbucks.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji walking toward a white historic wooden building with green window frames" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:32 — Momiji was curious too. This turned out to be a Starbucks</figcaption></figure>

<p>It was a <strong>Starbucks</strong>.</p>

<p>According to the interpretive sign, this is the <strong>former Nagahama Quarantine Station, Stop No. 1</strong> — built after the cholera outbreaks of the Meiji era (1870s), now a nationally registered Tangible Cultural Property, relocated and restored here in March 2025.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/umi-no-koen-dogrun-history.jpg" alt="Interpretive sign explaining the history of the former Nagahama Quarantine Station" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Reading the history board. A Meiji quarantine station reborn as a coffee shop — I like this timeline</figcaption></figure>

<p>Coffee in a 19th-century quarantine station. As someone who works in healthcare, there's something quietly moving about a public-health building retiring into a café.</p>

<h2 id="matome">Final Thoughts</h2>

<p>The dog run at Umi no Koen: two free, no-reservation areas under the tracks at Minamiguchi Station — and on a rainy Sunday at 1 p.m., completely ours.</p>

<p>Momiji's water-splashing plan was blocked by the summer beach ban, so that's postponed. But we found a place worth coming back to in September — as a scouting day, mission accomplished.</p>

<p>On the way home we stopped at <strong>Yokohama Nanbu Market</strong>, where I scored half-price wagyu beef offal, and my wife drove home while munching on little fried rockfish. That market is seriously cheap. If it were in our neighborhood, we'd be regulars.</p>

<p>Even on a day when the main plan falls through, you come home with a free dog run, a Meiji-era Starbucks, and half-price wagyu. That's what dog outings are really made of, I think.</p>

<p>Come September, I'll be back to add a photo of Momiji at the waterline.</p>

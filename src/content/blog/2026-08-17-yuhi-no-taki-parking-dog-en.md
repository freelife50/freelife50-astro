<p><strong>Reading time:</strong> about 7 minutes</p>

<p>Hi, I'm Hiroshi.</p>

<p>To escape the summer heat, we drove to <strong>Yuhi-no-Taki Falls</strong> in Minamiashigara, Kanagawa. Me, my wife, and Momiji our Shiba Inu.</p>

<p>We had been there once before on a hot day and it felt wonderful. So we thought, let's do it again.</p>

<p>…And then <strong>every parking space was full.</strong></p>

<p>But the best part of that day turned out to be not the famous waterfall. It was a small stream we found on the way there. Let me be honest about all of it.</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>People who want to know how crowded Yuhi-no-Taki gets in summer, and what to do if parking is full</li>

<li>Anyone looking for a quiet place near the water to cool off</li>
</ul>

<p>For reference, the city of Minamiashigara lists <strong>three free parking lots</strong> for the falls: Takishita (about 30 cars, 3-minute walk), Chojabashi (about 20 cars, 10 minutes), and Jizodo (about 30 cars, 15 minutes). The city also states clearly that summer, weekends, holidays and long vacation periods get crowded.</p>

<p>So the full lot was not bad luck. It was exactly what the city warns you about.</p>

<h2 id="parking">Does the Yuhi-no-Taki parking lot fill up? (1 p.m. on a Saturday)</h2>

<p>Short answer: <strong>yes, it does.</strong></p>

<p>This was Saturday, August 9, 2026, right around 1:00 p.m. — the weekend just before Japan's Obon holiday week.</p>

<p>As we drove up the mountain road, the cars were already packed in. Circling around hunting for a space felt rude to everyone else, so we gave up.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-parking-v3.jpg" alt="A handwritten sign reading 500 yen per day at a small private parking area near Yuhi-no-Taki Falls" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Where we ended up. That handwritten "500 yen per day" sign was a relief</figcaption></figure>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-parking-lot.jpg" alt="Cars parked on grass in a mountain valley under a blue summer sky" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The view from where we parked. The air was already cooler up here</figcaption></figure>

<p>One thing worth knowing: <strong>the falls themselves cost nothing to visit, and the parking is free.</strong> The three free lots were simply all taken.</p>

<p>The city's advice is to move on to Chojabashi or Jizodo if Takishita is full. That is a perfectly sensible plan.</p>

<p>But we had a dog with us, in the middle of a Japanese summer. I did not want to make her walk any farther than necessary on hot pavement.</p>

<h2 id="500yen">The backup plan: a private lot for about $3</h2>

<p>A little further down the road there was a private lot with a hand-written sign: <strong>500 yen per day, about $3.</strong></p>

<p>We had come all this way, so let's at least go cool off. Three dollars did not sting at all.</p>

<p>Then we got out of the car and <strong>two guard dogs</strong> started barking at us.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-banken.jpg" alt="Two dark-coated guard dogs standing under a tarp at the parking area" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The two dogs who came out from under the tarp. Momiji locked onto them immediately</figcaption></figure>

<p>Momiji got excited too, staring right back at them. There was a whole conversation happening between those dogs that I did not understand.</p>

<p>If you are bringing a dog, this is a moment to stay alert. I shortened the leash and we kept walking.</p>

<h2 id="branch">There is a path branching down to the left</h2>

<p>Walking from the lot toward the falls, I spotted a <strong>small path dropping away to the left.</strong></p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-branch.jpg" alt="A paved road toward the falls with a small path branching downhill to the left" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">On the left here, just before the guardrail, the ground drops away</figcaption></figure>

<p>I looked over the edge, and it seemed to lead <strong>straight down to the river.</strong> A mossy concrete track disappearing into the trees.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-path.jpg" alt="A mossy path descending through cedar trees toward a stream" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">You can already see water from up here. That was the moment I got excited</figcaption></figure>

<p>The parking situation told me the falls would be packed. So — let's try this way instead.</p>

<p>That one decision made the whole day.</p>

<h2 id="river">Can you bring a dog into the water? The stream where Momiji ran wild</h2>

<p>The stream at the bottom was <strong>fantastic.</strong></p>

<p>Shallow, clear enough to see the bottom, sunlight flickering across the surface through the leaves — and <strong>nobody there.</strong> We had it to ourselves.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yuhi-no-taki-parking-dog-stream.jpg" alt="A shallow stream surrounded by summer greenery, with a small Shiba Inu in the distance" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">All of this, and we were the only ones in it</figcaption></figure>

<p>Momiji loves playing in water. She waded into the shallows, splashed around, and then <strong>took off running like she had lost her mind.</strong></p>

<p>A couple in their fifties, cheering at a dog in a river, taking photos and videos non-stop. If anyone else had been there, it would have been embarrassing.</p>

<h2 id="honest">Full disclosure: I never actually saw the falls that day</h2>

<p>I'm not going to hide this.</p>

<p><strong>I did not see Yuhi-no-Taki Falls that day.</strong></p>

<p>We got absorbed in that little stream, and 50 minutes later, at 1:55 p.m., we were already walking back.</p>

<p>I had also wanted to try <em>takigyo</em> — standing under the falls, a traditional practice. That will have to wait too. Weekdays look far quieter, so I'll go back then.</p>

<p>Still, the takeaway from the day is clear.</p>

<p><strong>The best thing I found was not the waterfall. It was the stream on the way to it.</strong></p>

<p>Sometimes you fail to reach your destination and find something better than the destination. It's oddly reassuring that this still happens to you in your fifties.</p>

<h2 id="caution">Three things to know before you go (bears, floods, leeches)</h2>

<p>I don't want to leave this at "it felt great," so here is what I found when I looked into it.</p>

<p><strong>1. There has been a bear sighting.</strong><br>
The city of Minamiashigara reported that on the evening of <strong>July 1, 2026, one bear was sighted near the tunnel at Jizodo</strong> — which is the access point for Yuhi-no-Taki Falls. The city advises avoiding early morning and night, carrying a bear bell or otherwise making your presence known, never running away, and taking extra care along rivers and in thick brush. Worth checking the city's latest notices before you set out.</p>

<p><strong>2. Rising water after rain.</strong><br>
This is a mountain stream fed from the outer rim of the Hakone caldera. I could find no published water-level threshold for the falls, but this is exactly the kind of terrain where you stay out of the water during heavy rain, thunderstorms, or right after a downpour.</p>

<p><strong>3. Footing and leeches.</strong><br>
Visitor reviews mention mud remaining even on clear days, and a posted warning about leeches on site. Shoes with grip and long trousers are the safer choice.</p>

<p>And one dog-specific note: mid-summer asphalt gets brutally hot. The fact that our walk to the water was only about four minutes made a real difference for Momiji.</p>

<p>Car navigation can get confused in the mountains, so <strong>set your destination before you leave.</strong> Visitors also report that the road narrows near the end, with sections where passing another car is difficult.</p>

<p>One last thing about the name. "Yuhi" means evening sun, but going at sunset is not actually the best plan. One explanation of the name is a phenomenon in <strong>mid-January when the sun sets into the notch at the top of the falls</strong> — and when the tourism association photographed it in January 2026, the best timing was <strong>just after 11 a.m.</strong> Show up at dusk expecting golden light and you may find nothing but mountain shadow.</p>

<h2 id="matome">Closing thoughts</h2>

<p>At 1 p.m. on a summer Saturday, the free parking at Yuhi-no-Taki Falls was full.</p>

<p>We paid about $3 to park further down, turned left onto a path on the way to the falls, and found a stream where our dog ran around like she had lost her mind. That was the whole day.</p>

<p>Usually a day when you can't get where you were going gets filed under "well, that was a bust."</p>

<p>But because we couldn't get in, we went down to that stream. And the reason nobody else was there was, most likely, that everyone else had gone to the waterfall.</p>

<p>I'll go back for the falls on a weekday. And for the <em>takigyo</em>, eventually.</p>

<p>When a day refuses to go to plan, it's worth glancing down the side paths. One day like that, in one summer, seems like plenty.</p>

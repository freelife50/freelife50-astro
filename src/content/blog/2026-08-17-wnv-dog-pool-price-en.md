<p>Hi, I'm Hiroshi.</p>

<p>It was a hot day, but I figured a dog run would be manageable, so I took Momiji, our Shiba Inu, to <strong>Wonderful Nature Village</strong> in Akiruno, on the western edge of Tokyo.</p>

<p>We had been here before. This time, though, I wanted to try the <strong>dog pool</strong> — Momiji loves water more than almost anything.</p>

<p>At the front desk they asked, "Will you be using the pool as well?" I said yes right away. And the staff member replied with a very cheerful "Thank you very much!"</p>

<p>…That cheerfulness made me slightly nervous. My instinct turned out to be right.</p>

<h2 id="price">The dog pool is about $17 per dog, and yes, I flinched</h2>

<p>The number at the desk was <strong>2,500 yen — about $17 — for Momiji to use the pool.</strong></p>

<p>That is separate from admission. So: my entry fee, her entry fee, the parking fee, and then another $17 on top.</p>

<p>My honest first thought: <strong>that's expensive.</strong></p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-pool.jpg" alt="The dog pool at Wonderful Nature Village, a shallow blue pool with shade sails overhead" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The dog pool. The near edge is shallow, so nervous swimmers can wade in</figcaption></figure>

<p>Seeing it in person does explain the price. Maintaining a dedicated dog pool this size, this clean, is not a small thing.</p>

<p>Officially, the all-breeds "Dog Beach" is 280 m² with a <strong>maximum depth of 60 cm</strong>, and the "Doggy's Pool" for dogs under 8 kg is 40 m² at <strong>30 cm</strong> deep. Both slope gradually, so first-timers can ease in.</p>

<p>One thing worth knowing in advance: <strong>humans cannot get in the water.</strong> This is not the facility being stingy — it follows public health office guidance, and owners are barred except in an emergency. The pool is for dogs only.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-shade.jpg" alt="Shade sails stretched over the poolside area" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Shade sails over the poolside. In August you appreciate every square meter of it</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="vaccine">Forget these papers and you don't get in</h2>

<p>This is the part most likely to save someone a wasted trip.</p>

<p>I photographed the "First-time visitors" sign at the entrance.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-vaccine.jpg" alt="Entrance sign at Wonderful Nature Village showing sample rabies and combination vaccine certificates" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The entrance sign: without both certificates, your dog cannot enter</figcaption></figure>

<p>You need both of these:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>A <strong>rabies vaccination certificate</strong></li>
<li>A <strong>combination vaccine certificate (3-way or more)</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>Both must be <strong>within one year of the vaccination date, or still valid</strong>. The sign is blunt about it: if you cannot present both, the dog does not come in.</p>

<p>Driving all the way to Akiruno and then having nowhere to put your dog is not a scenario you want. Keep them in your wallet, or at least photograph them on your phone.</p>

<p>Payment-wise, major credit cards, transit IC cards and QR payments like PayPay were all accepted. Parking is generally cash, so keep some coins.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-rules.jpg" alt="A sign listing the park's rules and prohibited actions" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The rules board next to it. Retractable leashes, dogs on chairs, and more, all spelled out</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="card">The "village card" saves you the paperwork every visit</h2>

<p>Look closely at the sign and there are two routes at reception:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>A: Get the village card</strong> (500 yen, about $3 issuing fee) → no need to present certificates or fill in the consent form on future visits, plus visit points</li>
<li><strong>B: Skip the card</strong> → present certificates and fill in the consent form every single time</li>
</ul>

<p>If you expect to come back a few times a year, the $3 pays for itself in saved time at the desk. For a one-off visit, B is fine.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-gate.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji walking toward the park entrance gate" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Paperwork done. Note the spring in her step</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="pool">What my dog actually did at the pool</h2>

<p>Seventeen dollars for the pool. You would expect some serious swimming.</p>

<p>What Momiji actually did was <strong>work the crowd of other owners.</strong></p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-pool-momiji.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji being petted at the poolside" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">People over dogs. That has always been her ranking</figcaption></figure>

<p>Our dog prefers humans to other dogs. She walked the poolside charming every owner she could find.</p>

<p>She was also, unmistakably, delighted. And watching that, I let the money go.</p>

<p><strong>Good for you, Momiji. Worth every yen.</strong></p>

<p>…Honestly, the whole idea of "getting my money's worth" is the wrong frame. Dogs don't calculate value. They're either happy or they're not.</p>

<h2 id="dogrun">The dog run is huge — but a summer afternoon is hard on a dog</h2>

<p>After the pool we moved over to the dog run.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-dogrun.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji on a wide grassy dog run" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The grass run. She made full use of it</figcaption></figure>

<p>She tore around — and then the heat caught up with her.</p>

<p>Being honest: <strong>a mid-summer afternoon is genuinely tough on a dog.</strong> My "a dog run should be fine" assessment was a little optimistic.</p>

<div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">

<p>The video shows the scale better than a photo does.</p>

<p>Officially there are <strong>seven dog runs</strong>. The publicly available ones are the all-breeds area at 1,750 m², medium/large dogs at 510 m², small dogs at 390 m², a covered area at 400 m², and a beginners' area — plus two private-hire areas.</p>

<p>The one to remember is the <strong>covered 400 m²</strong>. On a rainy day, or a blazing one like this, that is where you relocate.</p>

<p>Also worth knowing: in 2026 the park ran <strong>evening hours until 8 p.m. from July 24 to August 31</strong>, with entry after 4:30 p.m. dropping to 450 yen (about $3) per adult and per dog, with free parking. For a Japanese summer, that is the more sensible slot for both species. The trade-off is that the restaurant, the hiking trail, and some dog runs close at 5 p.m.</p>

<h2 id="facility">The best-equipped dog facility I have been to</h2>

<p>This is, admittedly, an upmarket dog run.</p>

<p>What struck me, though, is that the other visitors matched it — a notably <strong>well-mannered crowd</strong>. There is a cafe, and the whole place has a polished feel.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/wnv-dog-pool-price-path.jpg" alt="A tidy planted walkway inside the park" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The grounds are properly maintained. Pleasant just to walk</figcaption></figure>

<p>There is also a hiking trail (about 2 km round trip, wood-chip surfaced), and a dog-friendly cafe restaurant called AZEKIRI where dogs are allowed <strong>inside</strong>, not just on the terrace.</p>

<p>There is even a self-service dog wash facility, so you don't have to load a wet, muddy dog straight into your car.</p>

<p><strong>It is the most complete dog facility I have visited.</strong> The point is not just running your dog — it's running, washing, eating, and even camping in one place.</p>

<h2 id="matome">Closing thoughts</h2>

<p>Seventeen dollars for the dog pool. At the desk, I flinched.</p>

<p>And what my dog did in that pool was charm strangers. Not much swimming involved.</p>

<p>Driving home, though, I found I didn't mind.</p>

<p>She was happy. It turns out that's the whole calculation.</p>

<p>Whether $17 is expensive isn't really decided by your wallet or by the facility's amenities. It's decided by the face your dog is making, asleep in the back of the car on the way home. Hers was the right face.</p>

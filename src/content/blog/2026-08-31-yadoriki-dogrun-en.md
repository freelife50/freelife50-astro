<p>Hi, this is Hiroshi from "Ramen, Beer, and Sometimes a Blog."</p>

<p>We hadn't taken our Shiba Inu Momiji out for a proper adventure in a while, and she was starting to sulk about it. So I texted my wife: "Dog run this weekend?" She texted back: "Let's go to that dog run in Yadoriki, Matsuda Town."</p>

<p>Here's the thing — I was thinking of the exact same place. We've been married long enough to read each other's minds, apparently. Newtype telepathy, achieved in our 50s.</p>

<p>So on Sunday, August 30, 2026, we drove out to <strong>Yadoriki Nanatsuboshi Dog Run</strong> ("Nanatsuboshi" means "seven stars") in the foothills of the Tanzawa mountains, Kanagawa Prefecture. Short version: the facilities are impressive, and the price is surprisingly gentle. If your dog loves water, keep reading.</p>

<h2 id="access">Where Is It? Access and Parking</h2>

<p>The address is Yadoriki 5605, Matsuda Town, Ashigarakami District, Kanagawa. It sits at the foot of the Tanzawa mountain range. Even Japanese people can't read the place name "寄" as "Yadoriki" on the first try, by the way.</p>

<p>My wife drove us from our home in Sagamihara in <strong>about an hour</strong>, mostly on local roads. If you're using expressways, it's about 15 minutes from Shin-Hadano IC (Shin-Tomei) or 20 minutes from Oi-Matsuda IC (Tomei).</p>

<p>Let me be honest: this place is <strong>deep in the mountains</strong>. The final stretch is a narrow mountain road, and while there's technically a bus stop (Yatoguchibashi) a 3–4 minute walk away, with a dog in tow a car is the only realistic option.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-parking.jpg" alt="Sign at the parking entrance of Yadoriki Nanatsuboshi Dog Run with an arrow pointing to the parking lot" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">12:45, arrival. Parking is 500 yen per car, roughly 50 spaces. Plenty of room even on a Sunday</figcaption></figure>

<p>It had just rained, the air was cool — hard to believe it was late August. Perfect dog run weather for Momiji, who wilts in the heat.</p>

<h2 id="price">Fees, Membership, and the Rabies Certificate You Must Not Forget</h2>

<p>Here's the fee board I photographed at the reception.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-price.jpg" alt="Fee board at Yadoriki Nanatsuboshi Dog Run: adults 330 yen, children 220 yen, dogs 440 yen, shower and blower 100 yen per 10 minutes, parking 500 yen" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Adults 330 yen, children 220 yen, dogs 440 yen. Warm-water shower & blower: 100 yen per 10 minutes. Open 9:00–17:00 (Mar–Sep), 9:00–16:00 (Oct–Feb), closed Tuesdays</figcaption></figure>

<p>Our bill: <strong>two adults 660 yen + Momiji 440 yen + parking 500 yen = 1,600 yen</strong>. Considering what's inside — you'll see below — that's a bargain.</p>

<p>One important warning. <strong>Membership registration is required</strong>, and you must show <strong>proof of rabies vaccination within the past year</strong> (the certificate, the tag, or a vet's exemption letter). No certificate, no entry. Imagine driving an hour up a mountain road and getting turned away at the gate. Check your bag before you leave home.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-member.jpg" alt="Information board about new membership registration and renewal, stating rabies vaccination documents are required" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The membership board at reception. Rabies documentation is required for both new registrations and renewals</figcaption></figure>

<p>I had a small flashback at the entrance. At <a href="/wnv-dog-pool-price-en/">Wonderful Nature Village in Akiruno, the dog pool alone cost 2,500 yen per dog</a>. So I braced myself here — and found there's no pool fee line item at all. Just people and dog admission. I actually said "ooh, nice!" out loud.</p>

<h2 id="area">Eight Fenced Areas: The Layout</h2>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-entrance.jpg" alt="Entrance gate with an arch reading Yadoriki Fureai Dog Run" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">12:55, the entrance gate. The moment we pass under the arch, Momiji's pace changes. She knows</figcaption></figure>

<p>Inside, it's much bigger than I expected — about 11,000 square meters in total, divided into <strong>eight fenced areas</strong> by dog size and temperament: a beginners' area, small dogs, small-to-medium (under 15 kg), medium-to-large (15 kg and over), two free-for-all areas, a roofed area, and a reservation-priority area.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-map.jpg" alt="Facility map of Yadoriki Nanatsuboshi Dog Run showing eight areas, the pool and the water play area" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The facility map — pool, stream and café all marked. You won't get lost on your first visit</figcaption></figure>

<p>If your dog is new to dog runs and nervous around big dogs, the beginners' area is a godsend. Momiji, a Shiba under 15 kg, can use the small-to-medium areas and the free areas. This "there's always an escape route" design really lowers the owner's blood pressure.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-area.jpg" alt="Gate of the medium and large dog area with a sign indicating 15 kg and over" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The medium/large dog area gate. The dividing line is 15 kg</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="pool">The Free Pool: Momiji's Splash Time</h2>

<p>Now for the main event. Momiji's beloved pool. Momiji — it's splash o'clock!</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-pool.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji standing in the shallow end of a bowl-shaped dog pool" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:05, pool time. A bowl-shaped pool of natural spring water that deepens gently toward the center</figcaption></figure>

<p>Momiji, naturally, goes berserk. Her face is one big grin the entire time. A dog, visibly laughing. The deep middle scares her, so her style is sprinting laps around the shallows at full throttle. That works too, girl.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-pool-run.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji running through the shallow water, splashing" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Maximum tension Momiji, shallow-end specialist</figcaption></figure>

<p>Other dogs were enjoying it too. One big dog just stood chest-deep in the water, motionless, cooling off — honestly indistinguishable from a middle-aged man soaking in a cold bath at a sauna. Living the dream, buddy.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-pool-dogs.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji playing in the pool with a black dog" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">With a new black friend. <p>One thing to know: <strong>the weekend/holiday pool rule</strong>. To prevent crowding and accidents, one representative per family wears a numbered tag, and <strong>each family gets 10–15 minutes per turn</strong>. If tags are available and nobody's waiting, you can go again as many times as you like. On our rainy-morning Sunday it was quiet, so we effectively had no waiting — but on a sunny midsummer weekend, brace yourself.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-pool-rule.jpg" alt="Notice stating pool use is restricted on weekends and holidays, with a numbered tag system limiting each family to 10 to 15 minutes" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The notice at the pool gate: weekends/holidays are tag-rotation, 10–15 minutes per family, repeatable when there's no queue</figcaption></figure>

<p>After the pool, a warm-water shower and blower station (100 yen per 10 minutes) lets you rinse and dry your dog before the drive home. No shampoo allowed — it's a rinse station, not a grooming salon.</p>

<h2 id="stream">The Stream: Tiny Canal Expedition</h2>

<p>Besides the pool, there's a small stream — more of a stone-lined canal — for water play. This thing is quietly excellent.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-stream.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji exploring a stone-lined water channel" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">13:50, the canal expedition sets off. Splish-splashing upstream through clear Tanzawa water</figcaption></figure>

<p>The mountain water stayed clear even after the rain. If your dog finds pools intimidating but loves water, this stream might be the perfect middle ground.</p>

<h2 id="run">The Lawn Area, Where Momiji Embarrasses Us</h2>

<p>Water cravings satisfied, we moved to the lawn runs. Several areas exceed 1,000 square meters — big dogs can hit full sprint with room to spare.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-run.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji standing on a wide lawn dog run" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">14:00, the lawn area. Momiji is in a great mood. And then, the incident</figcaption></figure>

<p>Here, Momiji does the thing. Our dog prefers humans to dogs — other dogs mostly annoy her. And yet <strong>she cannot resist stealing other dogs' balls</strong>. She charges after them with total commitment.</p>

<p>Stop. Please stop. Dad is begging you.</p>

<p>Worse, she barks at the dog who's rightfully chasing its own ball, trying to claim it mid-run. Cue apologetic bowing to the other owners. A fulfilling afternoon for Momiji; a mildly mortifying one for us.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-sunflower.jpg" alt="Shiba Inu Momiji walking past a flower bed of sunflowers and yellow flowers" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">Sunflower beds inside the park. The last Sunday of August — summer's closing scene</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="cafe">The Café and Events</h2>

<p>The on-site Nanatsuboshi Café serves dishes made with local Matsuda vegetables — and a proper <strong>dog menu</strong>, including venison. Soft-serve ice cream and shaved ice for the humans, "wanko soft-serve" for the dogs.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-cafe.jpg" alt="Menu board of the Nanatsuboshi Cafe with curry, drinks, and a dog menu" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">14:29, the café menu. Human menu and dog menu sharing the same wall. I like that arrangement</figcaption></figure>

<p>A poster advertised a 30-meter dash time-trial event for dogs, apparently a year-round series. This isn't just a fenced field — it's the kind of place that gets more fun the more you come back.</p>

<h2 id="matome">Final Thoughts</h2>

<p>Yadoriki Nanatsuboshi Dog Run cost our family — two adults, one dog, one car — <strong>1,600 yen</strong>, and for that we got eight fenced areas, a free natural spring-water pool and a stream, from 12:45 to 14:37. Nearly two hours of full-throttle dog joy. The only weakness is the remoteness. We can't come every week — but honestly, the mountain seclusion is half the charm. Nature, fully enjoyed. Dog, fully enjoyed.</p>

<p>On the drive home, Momiji slept like a log in the back seat. A fulfilling day for her; a slightly embarrassing one for her parents.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/images/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/yadoriki-dogrun-tai.jpg" alt="A whole sea bream about 30 cm long on a supermarket foam tray" /><figcaption class="wp-block-element-caption">The day's second trophy: a 30 cm sea bream for 598 yen. Salt-grilled it that night — magnificent</figcaption></figure>

<p>Postscript: at the supermarket that evening, I scored a <strong>whole sea bream for 598 yen</strong> — about 30 centimeters of fish. Salt-grilled, it was a triumph. The dog got her water; the old man got his fish. A very good Sunday.</p>
